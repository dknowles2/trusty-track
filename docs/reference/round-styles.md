# Round styles

A round everyone races can be run three ways. The Add Round dialog calls the
choice **How it's raced**; this page holds the full rules for each. For help
choosing, see
[Scoring & Championships](../scoring-and-championships.md#choosing-how-a-round-is-raced).

| | Schedule | Counts toward overall standings? | Ends when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Everyone races in every lane** | Made up front | Yes | Every heat has run |
| **Balanced** | Grows as results come in | Yes | Every car has raced the chosen number of times |
| **Elimination** | Grows as results come in | No — it has its own result | One car is left |

## Everyone races in every lane

The default, and the fairest comparison.

- Every car takes **each lane exactly once** per run. Lanes are not equal —
  small differences in the track make some faster — and this cancels that
  out.
- The schedule is one heat per car per run, and **every heat is full**: no
  car gets an easier day by racing in half-empty heats.
- **Runs per lane** multiplies the whole schedule: at 2, every car takes
  every lane twice. More runs produce fairer averages and take longer.
- The whole schedule exists before the first heat, so it can be
  [printed](printing.md#the-heat-sheet) and heats can be reordered.

## Balanced

Borrowed from GPRM, which calls it "Dynamic". The goal is that more
children win a heat.

- The first set of heats is drawn at random.
- Each later set ranks the cars — most heat wins first, then best average
  points per heat — and races **neighbours against neighbours**. The winners
  race the winners, which means the other heats are winnable.
- You choose how many times each car races. Once per lane is the usual
  advice and the suggestion offered.
- Within a heat, each car is given the lane it has used least. The matchups
  come first, so the lane spread is best-effort rather than exact.
- Everyone races the same number of times, so times and points **count
  toward the overall standings** as usual.
- New heats appear on their own once the current set has all been run. There
  is nothing to press.
- A racer who checks in late joins the next set. They will have raced fewer
  heats than everyone else, so in a points race the round is
  [set aside](scoring.md#rounds-that-are-set-aside).

## Elimination

The classic "lose too many and you're out" — with no bracket to draw and
nothing to reprint when somebody does not show up.

- A **loss is any heat a car does not win.** Second of four counts the same
  as fourth. A car that breaks down mid-heat takes a loss; a skipped heat is
  neither a win nor a loss.
- You choose how many losses a car is allowed. Three is a good default.
- New heats appear on their own once the current set has all been run. Cars
  are grouped by record — the undefeated race the undefeated — and **nobody
  races alone**: a leftover single car joins the nearest group's heat.
- A car at the loss limit is out. The **last car left wins.**
- Elimination heats **stay out of the overall standings** — an eliminated
  car races fewer heats, so no average or total over them is fair. The
  round's own page on Standings shows the result in losses: cars still
  racing first, then the eliminated in order of how long they lasted.
- A racer who checks in late joins the next set on a clean record — but
  never a race that is already decided. Checking in after the final heat
  does not restart it.
- An elimination round cannot also be a championship round. Its survivors
  *can* feed one: point a championship round at the elimination round and
  the last cars standing race a timed final. See
  [Championship rounds](championship-rounds.md#the-top-finishers-of-another-round).
